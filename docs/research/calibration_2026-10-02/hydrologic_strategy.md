# Hydrologic calibration strategy: evidence and experimental design

Research date: 2026-10-02. Repository inspected at `48f57fe1e65959d226cba3eafc6a001468276275`. This is a research recommendation, not evidence of a faster implementation. No engines were run and no production settings were changed.

## Recommendation

Retain DDS as a reference and first improve the problem it solves: version the objective, distinguish numerical validity from basin-specific process expectations, measure the cost of preparation and simulation, and then compare a bounded parallel optimizer portfolio. A surrogate-assisted search is the strongest next experimental candidate for reducing expensive evaluations; parallel independent searches are the simpler candidate for reducing elapsed time. Neither improvement is guaranteed for these basins.

Changing DDS to SCE-UA alone is not an evidence-based acceleration. Tolson and Shoemaker's original DDS study tested SWAT2000 problems with 6–30 dimensions and 1,000–10,000 evaluations. DDS outperformed SCE on their four expensive real calibration formulations. That is evidence for retaining a serious DDS comparator, not evidence that DDS will win for modern SWAT+ or the much smaller budgets used here. [Tolson and Shoemaker, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1029/2005WR004723).

## What the current implementation already does

The relevant paths are `calibration/locked_benchmark.py`, `calibration/real_engine.py`, `calibration/diagnostic_calibrator.py`, `full_mode/water_balance_gate.py`, `output/metrics.py`, and `docs/CALIBRATION_PARAMETER_REGISTRY.md`, under `src/swatplus_builder/` where applicable.

- The workflow is already phased: volume, baseflow/subsurface, peaks/timing, then KGE/NSE refinement. It already supports basin screening, warm-start anchors, bounded parallel screening, compact objective traces, integrity checks, and fresh locked verification. Simply proposing these features would duplicate existing work.
- The locked basin screen evaluates each supported parameter's lower and upper bounds separately against the generated model. Activity depends on the maximum absolute change in NSE or KGE. This is an endpoint probe, not a global interaction analysis. A parameter can affect low-flow or recession signatures without changing those aggregate metrics appreciably; a weak endpoint response does not prove global inactivity.
- `_score_candidate` incorporates `log_kge_v2` into refinement and baseflow objectives. Its comment describes the epsilon as scale-aware, but the known unit-conversion counterexample shows that changing the offset does not repair the log-KGE ratio pathology. The comment also invokes Pushpalatha et al. (2012) for a raw/log KGE blend that their low-flow criterion study does not establish.
- The candidate process gate removes only skill-threshold codes from the water-balance gate. Consequently ET/P > 0.70, the closure proxy, volume bias, and other non-skill codes can reject otherwise numerically valid candidates. These choices materially alter the feasible search region.
- The current closure implementation omits an explicit storage-change term although the gate module's introductory equation includes one. Treat its result as a documented diagnostic proxy until the engine's accounting boundary, internal transfers and storage terms are reconciled.
- A separate `autoresearch/surrogate.py` exists. Its relationship to the governed locked calibration interface should be assessed before adding another surrogate stack; existence does not establish correctness or suitability.

## Scientific prerequisites before optimizer comparisons

**Objective policy.** Define and seal a versioned policy using only the calibration period. Record the objective formula, metric versions, epsilon, selected dates, treatment of missing/negative/zero discharge, and undefined metric states. A non-finite required term must produce an explicit invalid evaluation rather than silently become a neutral bonus. Preserve the old objective for historical reproducibility; never mix old and new cached scores.

Two defensible alternatives to investigate are NSE on square-root discharge and NSE on `log(Q + epsilon)`, where `epsilon = c × mean(Q_observed)` is derived from the calibration observations and frozen. Santos et al. show why log-KGE should be avoided and discuss transformed NSE; square-root weighting is intermediate, while logarithms emphasize lower flows. Unit invariance does not establish that either objective is best for intermittent basins. Log-offset sensitivity and constant/all-zero observations still require explicit handling. [Santos et al., 2018](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/22/4583/2018/).

Do not call square-root NSE a dedicated low-flow criterion. Pushpalatha et al. compared criteria over 940 French catchments and found substantial high-flow contributions in many purported low-flow scores; they recommended inverse-flow NSE for stronger concentration on the lowest flows. That result supports checking which flow ranges carry objective weight, not adopting inverse flow without examining zero-flow behaviour. [Pushpalatha et al., 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2011.11.055).

For a practical first comparison, freeze raw KGE plus an explicitly weighted transformed-NSE component and report the components separately. Choose weights on development basins only, or compare predeclared objective policies as a separate experiment. Do not optimize the weights against the final held-out basins or temporal observations. Do not assert that an aggregate KGE blend gives equal attention to peaks and recessions without examining its actual error contributions.

Recent evidence also argues against a universal objective: a 2026 comparison used 47 conceptual models, 10 diverse CARAVAN catchments and eight objectives; no objective represented every signature best. This is conceptual-model evidence rather than a SWAT+ validation, but supports task-specific metric selection. [Wagener et al., 2026](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/30/4867/2026/).

**Validity versus soft process evidence.** Engine failure, malformed/non-finite outputs, provenance mismatch and contradictory routing accounting remain hard exclusions. ET/P > 0.70 is a policy threshold, not a universal conservation identity: substantial ET relative to precipitation can be compatible with a low-runoff basin. Likewise, a streamflow-derived BFI estimate is a filter-based signature, not a direct groundwater measurement. The current fallback BFI target of 0.55 is a prior and must be labelled as such.

Use independent, uncertainty-qualified ET, runoff partitioning or other process evidence to constrain parameters. Where such evidence is unavailable, retain the existing gate policy as an explicit comparator and test a proposed basin-context policy separately; do not silently relax claim gates to improve acceptance. Arnold et al. explain that good streamflow fit can coexist with incorrect internal processes and recommend joint hard/soft diagnostic calibration. Their emphasis is process representation and scenario credibility, not a demonstrated reduction in runtime. [Arnold et al., 2015](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/52655).

SWAT+ has native soft calibration using regional process targets and files such as `codes.sft`, `water_balance.sft` and `wb_parms.sft`. This could provide a process-consistent initialization before hard calibration. It changes parameters internally and requires capturing all resulting inputs as a new lock. Current documentation must be checked against the exact pinned engine source and executable before adoption. Targets need independent provenance and uncertainties; SWAT+ soft calibration is not a free substitute for gauged calibration. [Official SWAT+ soft-calibration documentation](https://docs.swat.tamu.edu/calibration/soft-calibration/).

## Ranked alternatives

| Priority | Candidate | Evidence and suitability | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bounded independent multi-start DDS or islands, with optional scheduled incumbent exchange | Closest to the current interface; each simulation stays isolated. Parallel DDS already has SWAT precedent. First test independent starts; compare sharing as an ablation. | Trades hardware for elapsed time and may spend more total CPU. Dividing a tiny budget among starts can weaken every search. |
| 2 | Surrogate-assisted DDS/DYCORS or an RBF proposal layer | Reuses exact evaluated candidates to propose promising, diverse points. Candidate for reducing real engine evaluations. Keep expensive-model confirmation authoritative. | Initial design cost, sparse data and discontinuous feasibility can defeat the surrogate. A surrogate score never establishes a claim. |
| 3 | Compact-population differential evolution | A clear population-based parallel comparator with simple bounded parameters. | Population initialization consumes budget; default population sizes can be unsuitable for expensive runs. No established win over DDS here. |
| 4 | CMA-ES on normalized, screened continuous parameters | Tests whether correlated parameter directions help, including interactions missed by staged coordinate changes. | Hard feasible boundaries and penalties can distort covariance learning; small budgets and many dimensions are difficult. |
| 5 | SCE-UA/SCE | Useful established hydrological comparator, especially at larger budgets. | Not the first acceleration recommendation because the original DDS SWAT evidence often favoured DDS. |
| 6 | Training-period surrogate windows or coarser spatial models | Potential per-evaluation cost reduction, but a separate fidelity experiment. | Warm-up cost, state consistency and rank reversals; changed HRUs/outlets/soils can change the actual modelling problem. |
| 7 | Basin-attribute/donor parameter transfer | Promising amortization across many future basins once a sufficient donor archive exists. | Must exclude test-basin observations, prevent upstream/downstream dependence, and demonstrate transfer under matched engine/forcing/parameter semantics. |

The priority ordering is an engineering and research judgment, not a ranking proven by head-to-head SWAT+ experiments.

**Parallel precedent.** Ercan et al. tested a cloud implementation of parallel DDS for SWAT across three watersheds and two simulation lengths. Asgari et al. subsequently proposed knowledge-sharing parallel DDS for distributed hydrologic models. These establish precedent for model-level parallelism and shared incumbents; their reported scaling should not be transferred to this workstation or pipeline. Record scheduling order and incumbent provenance because asynchronous completion can change a seeded trajectory. [Ercan et al., 2014](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/cee_facpub/1205/), [Asgari et al., 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1364815223000944?dgcid=author).

**Surrogate precedent.** PODS extends dynamic coordinate surrogate search and was tested on costly three-dimensional lake models, including a differential-evolution comparator. Gong et al. combine parameter screening, surrogate modelling and adaptive sampling for a single-column land-surface model. Both support testing a surrogate proposal layer; neither establishes SWAT+ performance or validates a global surrogate from the current sparse runs. Train separate continuous score models and feasibility models, include exploration, and retain uncertainty and novelty-distance information. [Xia et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envsoft.2020.104910), [Gong et al., 2015](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/19/2409/2015/).

**DE and CMA-ES.** DE was designed for nonlinear, non-differentiable continuous optimization and parallel evaluation. CMA-ES adapts a continuous sampling covariance. Their primary algorithm papers support treating these as suitable black-box candidates, not claiming superior hydrological efficiency. [Storn and Price, 1997](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1008202821328), [Hansen, 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00772).

**Sensitivity improvements.** Add a modest multi-location elementary-effects screen or an exploratory joint design, measuring volume, recession/low-flow and peak signatures as well as aggregate scores. Morris' design evaluates individually randomized OAT trajectories across the domain rather than relying on one reference model. With `d` parameters and `r` trajectories the basic design costs `r(d + 1)` evaluations; charge this cost to the calibration budget. Screening should prioritize dimensions and retain exploration, rather than permanently remove a parameter after a weak endpoint result. [Morris, 1991](https://doi.org/10.1080/00401706.1991.10484804).

**Fidelity safeguards.** Razavi and Tolson proposed mapping short-period model performance to the full calibration period rather than calibrating exclusively to a presumed representative short period. Their work explicitly warns that a short period's representativeness is not known in advance. For SWAT+, test paired low/full evaluations first, with identical initial-state rules and parameter semantics, quantify rank agreement and false rejection of strong full-period candidates, and retain full-period exploration. No cheap reusable restart state is assumed. [Razavi and Tolson, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/2012WR013442); an accessible author source is [Razavi's Waterloo dissertation](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/items/fa51029b-920d-4b10-a467-2fada95735ef).

**Parameter transfer.** Multi-basin calibration and parameter regionalization have established precedents. Bárdossy et al. separated catchment water-balance controls from transferable dynamics in conceptual models; Samaniego et al. calibrated transfer functions from physical attributes and validated on excluded catchments. These motivate donor warm starts or prior distributions, not direct averaging of effective SWAT+ scalar parameters. Different soil products, HRU resolutions and forcing sources alter parameter meaning. [Bárdossy et al., 2016](https://hess.copernicus.org/articles/20/2913/2016/), [Samaniego et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008WR007327).

## Benchmark that can support an efficiency claim

1. **Freeze the scientific problem.** Pin engine SHA, code SHA, forcing, soils, HRUs, outlet, management, observation alignment, initialization/warm-up, dates, parameter transformations/bounds, objective version and process policy. An optimizer run cannot repair or change these to gain apparent skill.
2. **Separate development from evaluation.** Use a small development group to choose hyperparameters and objective weights. Seal remaining basin identities and final temporal observations before testing. Previously inspected manuscript basins are development cases, not newly untouched scientific test data. Final verification can rerun identical locked parameters without scoring the held-out observations until search is finished.
3. **Profile the exact evaluator.** Measure preparation, engine, parsing, gate and cache times for valid and failed candidates. Record peak RAM and disk usage. Evaluate at 1, 2 and 4 workers initially; do not launch a large search before resource contention is understood.
4. **Compare in two complementary ways.** First equal expensive-evaluation budgets, then equal wall-clock budgets at fixed hardware. Count baseline, screening, warm starts, surrogate design, failed evaluations, repeated evaluations, final candidate checks and withheld verification; report these categories separately. Show quality-versus-evaluation and quality-versus-time curves, total CPU hours and valid-candidate fraction. Cached historical scores are not new engine evaluations, and reused evidence must be labelled.
5. **Use multiple stochastic trials.** Start with a small feasibility pilot, then predefine enough independent seeds to estimate variability across basins. Pair starting designs where the algorithms permit; a common seed number alone does not create equivalent initial conditions. Report distributions and paired basin differences, not the best seed.
6. **Protect held-out observations.** No sensitivity, donor choice, stopping rule, surrogate training, hyperparameter tuning, phase advancement or candidate selection uses them. If intermediate model selection needs validation, create an inner split within the training period. Compare final transfer once; repeated adjustment after viewing it turns it into development evidence.
7. **Assess scientific quality beyond the optimized score.** Report raw KGE components, NSE, volume bias, low-flow error/zero-flow occurrence, recession behaviour, peak timing/amplitude and finite process outputs. Evaluate independent ET or other process evidence when available. Retain parameter ensembles as behavioural alternatives, without calling them posterior uncertainty unless a probabilistic inference supports that interpretation.
8. **Use ablations.** Establish a repaired-objective DDS baseline; then add improved screening, independent parallel starts, sharing, and surrogate proposals separately. A full-system comparison with one ablation at a time distinguishes objective repairs from scheduling and proposal improvements. Historical DDS scores remain descriptive, not a fair comparator for a changed objective.
9. **Prespecify success.** Specify acceptable scientific-quality loss and runtime/evaluation improvement before results. Determine tolerances on development evidence and subject-matter needs. Report failures and unresolved engine defects, including non-finite ET; no optimizer is credited with fixing the engine merely because it avoids a pathological candidate.

## What would be a supportable contribution?

A new optimizer name or an agent selecting among DDS, DE and CMA-ES is insufficient. Phased calibration, soft constraints, parallel DDS, surrogates and parameter transfer all have precedents. A credible contribution could be an evidence-preserving adaptive allocation policy that selects which process-sensitive parameters and exact simulations deserve the remaining budget, while keeping validity exclusions, uncertain process information and independent verification distinct. Its novelty and benefit would need controlled comparisons against strong DDS and surrogate baselines across diverse new basins, under both runtime and evaluation budgets.

The initial paper should claim an implemented, audited strategy and measured cost–quality trade-offs only after those experiments. Until then, this document identifies hypotheses and a feasible route to test them.
